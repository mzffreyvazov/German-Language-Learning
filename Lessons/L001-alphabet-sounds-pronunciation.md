# L001 — German Alphabet, Sounds, and Pronunciation

## Goal
Master the German alphabet, vowel sounds (including umlauts ä, ö, ü), diphthongs (ei, ie, eu/äu), unique consonants and clusters (ch, sch, sp, st, ß), the German 'r' (uvular and vocalic), and syllable-final devoicing to read German words aloud accurately.

## Explanation

### 1. Vowels and Umlauts
German has standard vowels (a, e, i, o, u) and three umlauts (ä, ö, ü).
- **Vowel Length:** Vowels followed by a single consonant or silent 'h' are long (e.g. *Tag*, *Bahn*, *bieten*). Vowels followed by double consonants or clusters are short (e.g. *Bett*, *Kamm*, *Stadt*).
- **The Umlauts:**
  - **ö** (/øː/ or /œ/): Exactly like **ö** in Azerbaijani and Turkish (*göz*, *ördek*). Examples: *schön*, *Köln*.
  - **ü** (/yː/ or /ʏ/): Exactly like **ü** in Azerbaijani and Turkish (*gün*, *üzüm*). Examples: *über*, *Tür*.
  - **ä** (/ɛː/ or /ɛ/): Like **e** in Azerbaijani/Turkish (*ev*) or English *bed* / *fair*. Examples: *Mädchen*, *Käse*.

### 2. Diphthongs (Vowel Combinations)
- **ei** / **ai**: Pronounced like English "eye" or Azerbaijani/Turkish *ay*. Examples: *mein*, *nein*, *zwei*.
- **ie**: Pronounced as a long "ee" (/iː/) like English "see". Whichever letter is second determines the sound! Examples: *sie*, *hier*, *vier*.
- **eu** / **äu**: Both pronounced like "oy" in English *boy* (Azerbaijani/Turkish *oy*). Examples: *neu*, *Häuser*, *Euro*, *Deutschland*.
- **au**: Pronounced like "ow" in English *cow*. Examples: *Haus*, *Frau*.

### 3. Consonants and Consonant Clusters
- **w**: Always pronounced like English "v" (Azerbaijani/Turkish **v**). Example: *Wasser* ("Vasser").
- **v**: In native German words, pronounced like English "f" (*Vater*, *vier*). In foreign loanwords, like "v" (*Vase*).
- **z**: Always pronounced like "ts" (English *cats*, Azerbaijani/Turkish *ts*). Examples: *zwei* ("tsvai"), *Zimmer* ("tsimmer").
- **s**:
  - Voiced "z" before a vowel at syllable start (*Sonne*, *sehen*, *sie*).
  - Voiceless "s" at the end of a syllable or word (*das*, *Haus*).
- **ß** (*Eszett*): Always a voiceless sharp "s" (like *ss*). Examples: *Straße*, *groß*, *heiß*.
- **sch**: Pronounced like English "sh" (Azerbaijani/Turkish **ş**). Example: *Schule*.
- **sp-** and **st-**: At the start of a word or syllable root, pronounced "shp-" and "sht-". Examples: *Sport* ("shport"), *Stadt* ("shtatt"), *Stein* ("shtain").
- **ch**:
  - **ach-Laut** [x]: After *a, o, u, au*, deep in the throat, identical to Azerbaijani **x** (*xalq*). Examples: *Buch*, *Bach*, *Kuchen*.
  - **ich-Laut** [ç]: After *e, i, ä, ö, ü, ei, eu* and consonants, soft hissing sound like the softened Turkish/Azerbaijani palatal k in *peki*. Examples: *ich*, *Milch*, *Bücher*.
- **-ig** at word end: Pronounced like *-ich* in standard German (*richtig*, *wichtig*).

2. **das Buch** `[das buux]` — the book

5. **über** `[yy-ber]` — over / about
6. **der Wein** `[dehr vayn]` — the wine
7. **die Stadt** `[dee shtat]` — the city

### 1. The German "R" — Two Realizations
- **Consonantal R (Throat R):** At syllable beginnings or after consonants (*rot*, *Frau*, *groß*, *trinken*), pronounced in the throat (uvular [ʁ]), similar to French R or voiced Azerbaijani **x** / Turkish **ğ**. *(Rolled tip-of-the-tongue R as in Az/Tr is also understood everywhere and common in Bavaria/Austria).*
- **Vocalic R (Vowel R):** At word/syllable ends and in *-er* (*der*, *Wasser*, *über*, *hier*), vocalized into open vowel /ɐ/ (sounds like relaxed "ah" / "ə"). E.g., *Wasser* sounds like "Vass-ah".

### 2. The Letter "J"
- Always pronounced like English "y" (Azerbaijani/Turkish **y**). Example: *ja* ("ya" = yes), *Jahr* ("yahr" = year).

### 3. Syllable-Final Devoicing (Auslautverhärtung)
- Voiced consonants *b, d, g* become voiceless *p, t, k* at syllable ends: *Tag* sounds like "Tahk", *und* like "unt", *ab* like "ap".
